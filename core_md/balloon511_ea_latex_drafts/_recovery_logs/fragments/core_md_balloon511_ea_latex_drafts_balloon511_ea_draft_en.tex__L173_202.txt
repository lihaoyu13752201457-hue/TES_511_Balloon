\subsubsection{Prompt atmospheric source}
\label{subsec:prompt_source}

The background source model is divided into two lines, prompt atmospheric transport and delayed activation, in which the prompt atmospheric particle field is given by EXPACS/PARMA. PARMA3.0 is an analytical model parameterized from PHITS atmospheric-shower calculations and validated against terrestrial cosmic-ray fields, and EXPACS is its public tabulation/software interface \cite{Sato2015EXPACS,Sato2016PARMA4}. PHITS provides the underlying particle-transport physics: primary cosmic rays interact with atmospheric nuclei, the produced secondary hadrons, leptons, photons, and neutrons continue to transport in the atmospheric column, and the final flux is then parameterized by altitude, geomagnetic cutoff rigidity, solar modulation, energy, and direction. In this work, EXPACS/PARMA gives the incident differential flux varying with particle species, kinetic energy, zenith angle, geographic position, altitude, geomagnetic cutoff rigidity, and solar modulation. It is used only to construct the external radiation field; detector response, anticoincidence, topology selection, and mission-time counting are applied in later steps. The reference source definition in this work uses latitude $34^\circ$, longitude $100^\circ$, altitude $38\,\mathrm{km}$, vertical cutoff rigidity $R_c = 11.6\,\mathrm{GV}$, and the solar modulation condition $W = 118.3$ corresponding to 2025-08-31.

The prompt source includes photons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, protons, $\alpha$ particles, negative $\mu$ particles, and positive $\mu$ particles. For each particle family, the EXPACS/PARMA differential spectrum is converted into a MEGAlib far-field area source, covering the full zenith range $\theta = 0^\circ$--$180^\circ$ and the full azimuth range $\phi = 0^\circ$--$360^\circ$. The zenith-angle dependence is represented by 20 equal-$\mu$ differential angular bins, where $\mu = \cos\theta$, and the solid angle of each bin is $\Delta\Omega = 0.6283185307\,\mathrm{sr}$. The first 10 bins represent down-going particles ($0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 90^\circ$), and the last 10 bins represent up-going or albedo components ($90^\circ \leq \theta \leq 180^\circ$). Both the prompt transport and activation-production transport retain these two hemispheres, so upward albedo particles are not discarded during source construction. It is worth mentioning that the discrete energy spectrum output by expacs skips over the 511keV point, and an atmospheric 511keV background is additionally introduced in the source in the simulation.

The integrated source-plane fluxes are approximately $4.80$, $0.462$, $0.199$,
$0.117$, $0.112$, $0.0115$, $0.0050$, and $0.0056\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$
for $\gamma$, $n$, $e^-$, $e^+$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\mu^-$, and $\mu^+$,
respectively; the non-photon families together carry about
$0.91\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, or 16\% of the total. If Monte Carlo counts
were allocated strictly in proportion to flux, the detector and activation
statistics of these minority families would be poor, so the non-photon
families are generated with replicated samples and de-weighted in the rate
normalization. All particles are then transported through the
detector/cryostat mass model with Geant4/MEGAlib
\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016,Zoglauer2006}; the prompt run and the
activation-production run each generated 25,210,216 incident primaries. Each
particle species is normalized with its own transported exposure,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{event},j}=\left(\sum_i \mathcal{T}_{ij}\right)^{-1},
\end{equation}
