   155	For this purpose, the diffraction probability of photons by the crystal is no longer superposed onto photons as an efficiency parameter, but is defined as a discrete process at the Geant4 application layer \cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016}. The process takes the diffraction probability $R(|\Delta\theta|)$ according to the angular deviation of the current photon relative to the crystal plane, and converts it into a finite equivalent diffraction mean free path $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}$, so that the accumulated diffraction probability when the photon crosses a crystal path $\ell$ reproduces this value: $1-\exp(-\ell/\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}})=R(|\Delta\theta|)$, namely $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}=-\,\ell/\ln[1-R(|\Delta\theta|)]$; to avoid double counting with standard photoelectric absorption, $R$ is first normalized by the unabsorbed fraction ($R/(1-A)$) and then converted into a mean free path. After this mean free path is returned to Geant4, the diffraction process and the standard electromagnetic processes (photoelectric absorption and Compton scattering) each sample interaction lengths in the same competition framework, and the shortest one decides which process occurs first, rather than forcibly intercepting photons at the crystal boundary. Photons not selected to undergo diffraction still continue transport according to the standard electromagnetic physics, and may be absorbed, scattered, or directly transmitted.
   156	
   157	The diffraction probability is preferentially given by interpolation in an external XOP/CRYSTAL rocking-curve map as a function of angular deviation: this curve is calculated offline once in the preprocessing stage by the CRYSTAL/diff\_pat module of the XOP toolkit (called through xoppylib, with DABAX crystallographic data as input) \cite{SanchezDelRio2011XOP}, and gives the Ge(111) reflectivity/transmissivity/absorption curves as a function of angular deviation; during transport, the angular deviation is calculated in real time for every step inside the crystal, and the current diffraction probability is obtained by interpolation on that curve, namely ``offline library tabulation, probability used by angle during transport''. The mosaic orientation is implemented by applying a Gaussian angular perturbation to the ideal Bragg-plane normal, with the perturbation width given by the 30 arcsec mosaic FWHM; after diffraction occurs, the outgoing direction is mirror-reflected according to the perturbed plane normal, and the photon energy is unchanged. This split in which the ``process class only handles the transport interface, and the independent model class handles probability and direction decisions'' follows the idea of existing crystal Bragg-reflection extensions in Geant4 \cite{Guan2023BraggReflection,Reiazi2025G4BraggReflection}, and extends it to the energy band needed here.

V404 is a transient point source in the Galactic disk and is used here only as a reference. It is constructed using the megalib far-field source mode \cite{Zoglauer2006}; \textcolor{red}{the detailed construction method is given in Section 3.2.3 on point-source construction}. In addition to signal introduction, we also introduce the prompt cosmic-ray background component, also constructed in the far-field source mode, and introduce the activation-background construction. \textcolor{red}{The transport of the above three sources is simulated separately; in the three simulations, the particle flux, species, and energy crossing the plane where the focal plane is located are all intercepted as outputs; the three simulation outputs are normalized by equivalent observation time as the total output, and this total output can be separately input into the detector mass model as a parallel-beam source.}

   161	\subsection{Prompt atmospheric and delayed-activation source model}
   162	\label{sec:background}
   163	
   164	With the focused signal defined in Section~\ref{sec:optics}, the
   165	background side of the calculation requires two further source layers: the
   166	prompt atmospheric field incident on the payload, and the delayed radioactive
   167	population that this field builds up in the instrument. The two are
   168	constructed separately, so that source generation does not absorb assumptions
   169	that belong to detector selection: everything in this section defines incident
   170	particles or decaying nuclei, while vetoes, topology cuts, and the hard line
   171	window are applied only in Section~\ref{sec:selection}.
   172	
   173	\subsubsection{Prompt atmospheric source}
   174	\label{subsec:prompt_source}
   175	
   176	The prompt atmospheric field is derived from EXPACS/PARMA. PARMA3.0 is an
   177	analytical model parameterized from PHITS atmospheric-shower calculations and
   178	benchmarked against terrestrial cosmic-ray measurements, and EXPACS is its
   179	public tabulation and software interface
   180	\cite{Sato2015EXPACS,Sato2016PARMA4}. PHITS supplies the underlying
   181	particle-transport physics: primary cosmic rays interact with air nuclei, the
   182	secondary hadrons, leptons, photons, and neutrons are transported through the
   183	atmospheric column, and the resulting fluences are parameterized by altitude,
   184	geomagnetic cutoff, solar modulation, energy, and direction. In this work
   185	EXPACS/PARMA provides the differential incident flux as a function of particle
   186	species, kinetic energy, zenith angle, geographic position, altitude, cutoff
   187	rigidity, and solar activity, and is used only to construct the external
   188	radiation fields; detector response, vetoing, topology selection, and
   189	mission-time counting are applied downstream. The reference source definitions
   190	use latitude $34^\circ$, longitude $100^\circ$, altitude $38\,\mathrm{km}$,
   191	vertical cutoff rigidity $R_c=11.6\,\mathrm{GV}$, and the 2025-08-31 solar
   192	condition corresponding to $W=118.3$.
   193	
   194	The prompt source includes photons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, protons,
   195	alpha particles, and negative and positive muons. For each particle family the
   196	EXPACS/PARMA differential spectrum is converted into a MEGAlib far-field area
   197	source covering the full zenith range $\theta=0^\circ$--$180^\circ$ and full
   198	azimuth range $\phi=0^\circ$--$360^\circ$. The zenith dependence is represented
   199	by 20 differential equal-$\mu$ angular bins, where $\mu=\cos\theta$ and each
   200	bin subtends $\Delta\Omega=0.6283185307\,\mathrm{sr}$: the first ten bins cover
   201	down-going particles, the last ten up-going or albedo-like particles. Both
   202	hemispheres are retained in the prompt transport and in the
   203	activation-production transport, so upward albedo particles are not discarded
   204	at source construction. Figure~\ref{fig:expacs_fullsphere_flux} shows the
   205	resulting full-sphere flux field.

   214	The integrated source-plane fluxes are approximately $4.80$, $0.462$, $0.199$,
   215	$0.117$, $0.112$, $0.0115$, $0.0050$, and $0.0056\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$
   216	for $\gamma$, $n$, $e^-$, $e^+$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\mu^-$, and $\mu^+$,
   217	respectively; the non-photon families together carry about
   218	$0.91\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, or 16\% of the total. If Monte Carlo counts
   219	were allocated strictly in proportion to flux, the detector and activation
   220	statistics of these minority families would be poor, so the non-photon
   221	families are generated with replicated samples and de-weighted in the rate
   222	normalization. All particles are then transported through the
   223	detector/cryostat mass model with Geant4/MEGAlib
   224	\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016,Zoglauer2006}; the prompt run and the
   225	activation-production run each generated 25,210,216 incident primaries. Each
   226	particle species is normalized with its own transported exposure,
   227	\begin{equation}
   228	  r_{\mathrm{event},j}=\left(\sum_i \mathcal{T}_{ij}\right)^{-1},
   229	\end{equation}
   230	where $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ is the transported exposure of subsample $i$ for
   231	species $j$. This per-species normalization is required because the photon and
   232	non-photon streams have different sampling and exposure structures; the
   233	normalization check enforces $r_{\mathrm{event},j}\sum_i\mathcal{T}_{ij}=1$
   234	for every species, so over-sampling changes the Monte Carlo variance but not
   235	the expected physical rate.

   237	\subsubsection{Delayed activation source}
   238	\label{subsec:delayed_source}
   239	
   240	The activation-production stream is not counted as an immediate detector
   241	background. It records instead the isotope, the material volume, and the
   242	production position of every radioactive product. These records are converted
   243	into a fixed day-15 radioactive population using production--decay buildup,
   244	with ground-state half-lives checked against NUBASE2020
   245	\cite{Kondev2021NUBASE}. For isotope $k$,
   246	\begin{equation}
   247	  A_k(t_{\mathrm{flight}}) = P_k\left[1-\exp\left(-\lambda_k t_{\mathrm{flight}}\right)\right],
   248	  \qquad \lambda_k = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2,k}},
   249	\end{equation}
   250	where $P_k$ is the production rate inferred from the activation-production
   251	transport and $t_{\mathrm{flight}}=15\,\mathrm{d}$ for the reference state.
   252	The resulting fixed delayed activity used by the reference construction is
   253	$85.45\,\mathrm{Bq}$.
   254	
   255	The position information does not come from the aggregate isotope store alone.
   256	The standard store records volume names and isotope counts; the
   257	production-position construction adds a custom hook in the Geant4/MEGAlib
   258	stepping-action layer. At the moment an isotope is stored during activation
   259	buildup, the hook writes a production-position record containing the logical
   260	volume, the pre-step position $(x,y,z)$, the isotope identifier $1000Z+A$, the
   261	excitation energy, time, process, and track-ancestry metadata. The fixed
   262	day-15 population is then joined to these records by volume, isotope, and
   263	excitation state, which gives the delayed source access to the simulated
   264	production coordinates rather than only to volume-integrated activities.
   265	Figure~\ref{fig:delayed_position_distribution} in Appendix~\ref{app:delayed}
   266	shows the resulting position distribution: the activity-weighted map is
   267	dominated by CsI-shield activation and $^{128}$I, with smaller contributions
   268	from Cu, Al, W, Mg, and Cs isotopes. It is a source-construction diagnostic,
   269	not a detector-count map; detector response is applied only after the delayed
   270	decays are transported through the common geometry and event selection.
   271	
   272	Whether the production positions are worth preserving can be tested from the
   273	same table. Delayed decays are emitted locally, so downstream attenuation,
   274	active-shield veto probability, and event topology all depend on where the
   275	isotope was produced, and different incident families---with their different
   276	energy-loss, stopping, and capture histories---need not deposit their
   277	activation in the same materials. To quantify this, the weighted production
   278	rows are grouped by incident particle family and material category, and the
   279	activity-normalized category distributions of two families $a$ and $b$ are
   280	compared with the total-variation distance
   281	\begin{equation}
   282	  D_{\mathrm{TV}}(a,b) =
   283	  \frac{1}{2}\sum_c \left| f_{a,c} - f_{b,c} \right| ,
   284	\end{equation}
   285	where $f_{a,c}$ is the fraction of delayed activity from family $a$ produced
   286	in material category $c$. In the fixed day-15 table, neutron-induced products
   287	carry about $82.5\,\mathrm{Bq}$, $\mu^-$-induced products $2.75\,\mathrm{Bq}$,
   288	proton-induced products $0.15\,\mathrm{Bq}$, and $\alpha$-induced products
   289	$0.022\,\mathrm{Bq}$, with smaller $\mu^+$ and $e^+$ terms. The
   290	neutron-induced activity remains dominated by the CsI shield, whereas the
   291	$\mu^-$ component is qualitatively different: Cu/support material, Al shells,
   292	and W-bearing passive material account for 32\%, 29\%, and 21\% of the
   293	$\mu^-$-induced activity, respectively
   294	(Figure~\ref{fig:position_sampling_necessity} in Appendix~\ref{app:delayed}).
   295	A volume-integrated or axisymmetric radial source could preserve the total
   296	activity, but it would erase these incident-family, material, nuclide, and
   297	coordinate correlations; the production-position construction keeps them in
   298	the source before detector transport.
   299	
   300	The delayed-decay source is therefore launched from sampled production
   301	positions rather than from an axisymmetric profile. Let row $j$ of the
   302	weighted production table carry activity weight $w_j$, with total activity
   303	$A=\sum_j w_j$. The construction draws $M$ production positions with
   304	replacement using probabilities $p_j=w_j/A$ and assigns each sampled point
   305	source the flux $A/M$. The expected activity assigned to row $j$ is then
   306	\begin{equation}
   307	  \mathbb{E}\!\left[N_j\frac{A}{M}\right] = M p_j \frac{A}{M} = w_j ,
   308	\end{equation}
   309	so the estimator preserves the total activity and is unbiased for the weighted
   310	spatial distribution. Here $M$ is the number of equal-activity sampled point
   311	sources, a sampling size rather than the number of physical production rows.
   312	The reference delayed source preserves the fixed total activity at the
   313	source-definition level and is replayed through the same detector/cryostat
   314	geometry as the prompt stream; the nominal calculation transported $10^{6}$
   315	delayed decays, and the corresponding Cosima live time is a transport record
   316	for the generated decay sequence, not a physical balloon exposure.
