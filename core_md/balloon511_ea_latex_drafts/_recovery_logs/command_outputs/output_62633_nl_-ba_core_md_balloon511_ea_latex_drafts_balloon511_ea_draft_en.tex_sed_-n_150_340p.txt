   155	For this purpose, the diffraction probability of photons by the crystal is no longer superposed onto photons as an efficiency parameter, but is defined as a discrete process at the Geant4 application layer \cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016}. The process takes the diffraction probability $R(|\Delta\theta|)$ according to the angular deviation of the current photon relative to the crystal plane, and converts it into a finite equivalent diffraction mean free path $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}$, so that the accumulated diffraction probability when the photon crosses a crystal path $\ell$ reproduces this value: $1-\exp(-\ell/\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}})=R(|\Delta\theta|)$, namely $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}=-\,\ell/\ln[1-R(|\Delta\theta|)]$; to avoid double counting with standard photoelectric absorption, $R$ is first normalized by the unabsorbed fraction ($R/(1-A)$) and then converted into a mean free path. After this mean free path is returned to Geant4, the diffraction process and the standard electromagnetic processes (photoelectric absorption and Compton scattering) each sample interaction lengths in the same competition framework, and the shortest one decides which process occurs first, rather than forcibly intercepting photons at the crystal boundary. Photons not selected to undergo diffraction still continue transport according to the standard electromagnetic physics, and may be absorbed, scattered, or directly transmitted.
   156	
   157	The diffraction probability is preferentially given by interpolation in an external XOP/CRYSTAL rocking-curve map as a function of angular deviation: this curve is calculated offline once in the preprocessing stage by the CRYSTAL/diff\_pat module of the XOP toolkit (called through xoppylib, with DABAX crystallographic data as input) \cite{SanchezDelRio2011XOP}, and gives the Ge(111) reflectivity/transmissivity/absorption curves as a function of angular deviation; during transport, the angular deviation is calculated in real time for every step inside the crystal, and the current diffraction probability is obtained by interpolation on that curve, namely ``offline library tabulation, probability used by angle during transport''. The mosaic orientation is implemented by applying a Gaussian angular perturbation to the ideal Bragg-plane normal, with the perturbation width given by the 30 arcsec mosaic FWHM; after diffraction occurs, the outgoing direction is mirror-reflected according to the perturbed plane normal, and the photon energy is unchanged. This split in which the ``process class only handles the transport interface, and the independent model class handles probability and direction decisions'' follows the idea of existing crystal Bragg-reflection extensions in Geant4 \cite{Guan2023BraggReflection,Reiazi2025G4BraggReflection}, and extends it to the energy band needed here.

   161	\subsubsection{Reference point-source construction}
   162	\label{subsec:pointsource}
   163	
   164	The focused signal stream is defined by a reference astrophysical point
   165	source. As a concrete example of the target class we take the 511\keV{}
   166	emission episode of the microquasar V404 Cygni observed with INTEGRAL/SPI
   167	\cite{Siegert2016V404}; V404 Cygni is a transient point source in the Galactic
   168	disk and serves here only as a reference for the source class, not as a
   169	predicted target flux. The reference flux scale itself,
   170	$\fzero=10^{-4}\phcms$, is the above-atmosphere value motivated in
   171	Section~\ref{sec:requirements}.
   172	
   173	In the optics run, the source is a monochromatic 511\keV{} far-field beam:
   174	photons arrive parallel to the optical axis, as from a point source at
   175	astronomical distance, and are sampled uniformly over the incident aperture
   176	$A_{\mathrm{inc}}$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:laue_aeff}). Three independent seeds of
   177	$5\times10^{4}$ primaries each give $1.5\times10^{5}$ transported photons, of
   178	which 37,256 are diffracted and cross the focal plane; 37,194 of these
   179	(99.8\%) fall within the $r=1.898\,\mathrm{cm}$ Be entrance window, with
   180	focused-spot radii $r_{90}=1.03\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_{99}=1.25\,\mathrm{cm}$.
   181	The within-window crossings form the event list that is replayed through the
   182	Be aperture into the detector/cryostat model with the recorded positions and
   183	directions.
   184	
   185	The normalization ties this replay to the reference flux. At the detector
   186	entrance the focused stream carries the rate
   187	$\fzero\,\aeff(511\keV)\,T$, where $T=0.739$ is the reference 511\keV{}
   188	atmospheric transmission at the day-15 trajectory anchor
   189	(Section~\ref{sec:mission_time}); numerically this is
   190	$1.48\times10^{-3}\cps$ at the reference flux. Equivalently, the generating
   191	run corresponds to an observation time $N_{\mathrm{fp}}/[\fzero\,\aeff\,T]$,
   192	and each replayed event carries the inverse of that exposure as its rate
   193	weight; in the intermediate cut-flow bookkeeping of
   194	Section~\ref{sec:selection} the same sample appears as a unit replay before
   195	this scale is applied. After the common detector response and event selection,
   196	the surviving signal rate is $1.19\times10^{-3}\cps$, corresponding to a
   197	selected effective area of $S_{\wii}/\fzero=11.9\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Because the
   198	optics run contains only source photons, the replayed stream is signal-pure;
   199	all backgrounds enter through the streams of Section~\ref{sec:background} and
   200	the boundary terms of Section~\ref{subsec:upstream_optics_background}.

   202	\subsection{Prompt atmospheric and delayed-activation source model}
   203	\label{sec:background}
   204	
   205	With the focused signal defined in Section~\ref{subsec:pointsource}, the
   206	background side of the calculation requires two further source layers: the
   207	prompt atmospheric field incident on the payload, and the delayed radioactive
   208	population that this field builds up in the instrument. The two are
   209	constructed separately, so that source generation does not absorb assumptions
   210	that belong to detector selection: everything in this section defines incident
   211	particles or decaying nuclei, while vetoes, topology cuts, and the hard line
   212	window are applied only in Section~\ref{sec:selection}.
   213	
   214	\subsubsection{Prompt atmospheric source}
   215	\label{subsec:prompt_source}
   216	
   217	The prompt atmospheric field is derived from EXPACS/PARMA. PARMA3.0 is an
   218	analytical model parameterized from PHITS atmospheric-shower calculations and
   219	benchmarked against terrestrial cosmic-ray measurements, and EXPACS is its
   220	public tabulation and software interface
   221	\cite{Sato2015EXPACS,Sato2016PARMA4}. PHITS supplies the underlying
   222	particle-transport physics: primary cosmic rays interact with air nuclei, the
   223	secondary hadrons, leptons, photons, and neutrons are transported through the
   224	atmospheric column, and the resulting fluences are parameterized by altitude,
   225	geomagnetic cutoff, solar modulation, energy, and direction. In this work
   226	EXPACS/PARMA provides the differential incident flux as a function of particle
   227	species, kinetic energy, zenith angle, geographic position, altitude, cutoff
   228	rigidity, and solar activity, and is used only to construct the external
   229	radiation fields; detector response, vetoing, topology selection, and
   230	mission-time counting are applied downstream. The reference source definitions
   231	use latitude $34^\circ$, longitude $100^\circ$, altitude $38\,\mathrm{km}$,
   232	vertical cutoff rigidity $R_c=11.6\,\mathrm{GV}$, and the 2025-08-31 solar
   233	condition corresponding to $W=118.3$.
   234	
   235	The prompt source includes photons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, protons,
   236	alpha particles, and negative and positive muons. For each particle family the
   237	EXPACS/PARMA differential spectrum is converted into a MEGAlib far-field area
   238	source covering the full zenith range $\theta=0^\circ$--$180^\circ$ and full
   239	azimuth range $\phi=0^\circ$--$360^\circ$. The zenith dependence is represented
   240	by 20 differential equal-$\mu$ angular bins, where $\mu=\cos\theta$ and each
   241	bin subtends $\Delta\Omega=0.6283185307\,\mathrm{sr}$: the first ten bins cover
   242	down-going particles, the last ten up-going or albedo-like particles. Both
   243	hemispheres are retained in the prompt transport and in the
   244	activation-production transport, so upward albedo particles are not discarded
   245	at source construction. Figure~\ref{fig:expacs_fullsphere_flux} shows the
   246	resulting full-sphere flux field.

   255	The integrated source-plane fluxes are approximately $4.80$, $0.462$, $0.199$,
   256	$0.117$, $0.112$, $0.0115$, $0.0050$, and $0.0056\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$
   257	for $\gamma$, $n$, $e^-$, $e^+$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\mu^-$, and $\mu^+$,
   258	respectively; the non-photon families together carry about
   259	$0.91\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, or 16\% of the total. If Monte Carlo counts
   260	were allocated strictly in proportion to flux, the detector and activation
   261	statistics of these minority families would be poor, so the non-photon
   262	families are generated with replicated samples and de-weighted in the rate
   263	normalization. All particles are then transported through the
   264	detector/cryostat mass model with Geant4/MEGAlib
   265	\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016,Zoglauer2006}; the prompt run and the
   266	activation-production run each generated 25,210,216 incident primaries. Each
   267	particle species is normalized with its own transported exposure,
   268	\begin{equation}
   269	  r_{\mathrm{event},j}=\left(\sum_i \mathcal{T}_{ij}\right)^{-1},
   270	\end{equation}
   271	where $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ is the transported exposure of subsample $i$ for
   272	species $j$. This per-species normalization is required because the photon and
   273	non-photon streams have different sampling and exposure structures; the
   274	normalization check enforces $r_{\mathrm{event},j}\sum_i\mathcal{T}_{ij}=1$
   275	for every species, so over-sampling changes the Monte Carlo variance but not
   276	the expected physical rate.

   278	\subsubsection{Delayed activation source}
   279	\label{subsec:delayed_source}
   280	
   281	The activation-production stream is not counted as an immediate detector
   282	background. It records instead the isotope, the material volume, and the
   283	production position of every radioactive product. These records are converted
   284	into a fixed day-15 radioactive population using production--decay buildup,
   285	with ground-state half-lives checked against NUBASE2020
   286	\cite{Kondev2021NUBASE}. For isotope $k$,
   287	\begin{equation}
   288	  A_k(t_{\mathrm{flight}}) = P_k\left[1-\exp\left(-\lambda_k t_{\mathrm{flight}}\right)\right],
   289	  \qquad \lambda_k = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2,k}},
   290	\end{equation}
   291	where $P_k$ is the production rate inferred from the activation-production
   292	transport and $t_{\mathrm{flight}}=15\,\mathrm{d}$ for the reference state.
   293	The resulting fixed delayed activity used by the reference construction is
   294	$85.45\,\mathrm{Bq}$.
   295	
   296	The position information does not come from the aggregate isotope store alone.
   297	The standard store records volume names and isotope counts; the
   298	production-position construction adds a custom hook in the Geant4/MEGAlib
   299	stepping-action layer. At the moment an isotope is stored during activation
   300	buildup, the hook writes a production-position record containing the logical
   301	volume, the pre-step position $(x,y,z)$, the isotope identifier $1000Z+A$, the
   302	excitation energy, time, process, and track-ancestry metadata. The fixed
   303	day-15 population is then joined to these records by volume, isotope, and
   304	excitation state, which gives the delayed source access to the simulated
   305	production coordinates rather than only to volume-integrated activities.
   306	Figure~\ref{fig:delayed_position_distribution} in Appendix~\ref{app:delayed}
   307	shows the resulting position distribution: the activity-weighted map is
   308	dominated by CsI-shield activation and $^{128}$I, with smaller contributions
   309	from Cu, Al, W, Mg, and Cs isotopes. It is a source-construction diagnostic,
   310	not a detector-count map; detector response is applied only after the delayed
   311	decays are transported through the common geometry and event selection.
   312	
   313	Whether the production positions are worth preserving can be tested from the
   314	same table. Delayed decays are emitted locally, so downstream attenuation,
   315	active-shield veto probability, and event topology all depend on where the
   316	isotope was produced, and different incident families---with their different
   317	energy-loss, stopping, and capture histories---need not deposit their
   318	activation in the same materials. To quantify this, the weighted production
   319	rows are grouped by incident particle family and material category, and the
   320	activity-normalized category distributions of two families $a$ and $b$ are
   321	compared with the total-variation distance
   322	\begin{equation}
   323	  D_{\mathrm{TV}}(a,b) =
   324	  \frac{1}{2}\sum_c \left| f_{a,c} - f_{b,c} \right| ,
   325	\end{equation}
   326	where $f_{a,c}$ is the fraction of delayed activity from family $a$ produced
   327	in material category $c$. In the fixed day-15 table, neutron-induced products
   328	carry about $82.5\,\mathrm{Bq}$, $\mu^-$-induced products $2.75\,\mathrm{Bq}$,
   329	proton-induced products $0.15\,\mathrm{Bq}$, and $\alpha$-induced products
   330	$0.022\,\mathrm{Bq}$, with smaller $\mu^+$ and $e^+$ terms. The
   331	neutron-induced activity remains dominated by the CsI shield, whereas the
   332	$\mu^-$ component is qualitatively different: Cu/support material, Al shells,
   333	and W-bearing passive material account for 32\%, 29\%, and 21\% of the
   334	$\mu^-$-induced activity, respectively
   335	(Figure~\ref{fig:position_sampling_necessity} in Appendix~\ref{app:delayed}).
   336	A volume-integrated or axisymmetric radial source could preserve the total
   337	activity, but it would erase these incident-family, material, nuclide, and
   338	coordinate correlations; the production-position construction keeps them in
   339	the source before detector transport.
