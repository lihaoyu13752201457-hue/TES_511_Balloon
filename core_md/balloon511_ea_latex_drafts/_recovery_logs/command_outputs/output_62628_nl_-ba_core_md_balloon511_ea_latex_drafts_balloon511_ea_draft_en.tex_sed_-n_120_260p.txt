   142	\subsection{Laue optics and detector-coupled signal replay}
   143	\label{sec:optics}
   144	
   145	This simulation adopts a laue-lens system based on mosaic Ge(111) crystals \cite{Barriere2009Laue,Barriere2011LauePrototype}. Through optical design, it realizes a 10 m focal length, \textcolor{red}{a multi-ring system realizes the 450keV-550keV detection band}, and an effective area of 20cm**2 at 511keV; the effective area is calculated as
   146	\begin{equation}
   147	  A_{\mathrm{eff}}(E)=A_{\mathrm{inc}}\frac{N_{\mathrm{fp}}(E)}{N_{\mathrm{inc}}(E)}
   148	  \label{eq:laue_aeff}
   149	\end{equation}
   150	where $A_{\mathrm{inc}}$ is the Monte Carlo incident sampling area, $N_{\mathrm{inc}}(E)$ is the number of incident photons, and $N_{\mathrm{fp}}(E)$ is the number of photons satisfying the focusing/focal-plane interception condition.
   151	The mosaic system is adopted to make the Monte Carlo calculation convenient while satisfying the expected focusing performance; other candidate schemes are also possible for actual engineering adaptation.
   152	
   153	In this simulation, the laue optical branch gives the focusing-performance simulation and \textcolor{red}{also realizes particle transport of itself as mass}.
   154	
   155	For this purpose, the diffraction probability of photons by the crystal is no longer superposed onto photons as an efficiency parameter, but is defined as a discrete process at the Geant4 application layer \cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016}. The process takes the diffraction probability $R(|\Delta\theta|)$ according to the angular deviation of the current photon relative to the crystal plane, and converts it into a finite equivalent diffraction mean free path $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}$, so that the accumulated diffraction probability when the photon crosses a crystal path $\ell$ reproduces this value: $1-\exp(-\ell/\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}})=R(|\Delta\theta|)$, namely $\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}=-\,\ell/\ln[1-R(|\Delta\theta|)]$; to avoid double counting with standard photoelectric absorption, $R$ is first normalized by the unabsorbed fraction ($R/(1-A)$) and then converted into a mean free path. After this mean free path is returned to Geant4, the diffraction process and the standard electromagnetic processes (photoelectric absorption and Compton scattering) each sample interaction lengths in the same competition framework, and the shortest one decides which process occurs first, rather than forcibly intercepting photons at the crystal boundary. Photons not selected to undergo diffraction still continue transport according to the standard electromagnetic physics, and may be absorbed, scattered, or directly transmitted.
   156	
   157	The diffraction probability is preferentially given by interpolation in an external XOP/CRYSTAL rocking-curve map as a function of angular deviation: this curve is calculated offline once in the preprocessing stage by the CRYSTAL/diff\_pat module of the XOP toolkit (called through xoppylib, with DABAX crystallographic data as input) \cite{SanchezDelRio2011XOP}, and gives the Ge(111) reflectivity/transmissivity/absorption curves as a function of angular deviation; during transport, the angular deviation is calculated in real time for every step inside the crystal, and the current diffraction probability is obtained by interpolation on that curve, namely ``offline library tabulation, probability used by angle during transport''. The mosaic orientation is implemented by applying a Gaussian angular perturbation to the ideal Bragg-plane normal, with the perturbation width given by the 30 arcsec mosaic FWHM; after diffraction occurs, the outgoing direction is mirror-reflected according to the perturbed plane normal, and the photon energy is unchanged. This split in which the ``process class only handles the transport interface, and the independent model class handles probability and direction decisions'' follows the idea of existing crystal Bragg-reflection extensions in Geant4 \cite{Guan2023BraggReflection,Reiazi2025G4BraggReflection}, and extends it to the energy band needed here.
   158	
   159	Here the signal photons use as reference the point source of the V404 source once detected by SPI \cite{Siegert2016V404}. V404 is a transient point source in the Galactic disk and is used here only as a reference. It is constructed using the megalib far-field source mode \cite{Zoglauer2006}; \textcolor{red}{the detailed construction method is given in Section 3.2.3 on point-source construction}. In addition to signal introduction, we also introduce the prompt cosmic-ray background component, also constructed in the far-field source mode, and introduce the activation-background construction. \textcolor{red}{The transport of the above three sources is simulated separately; in the three simulations, the particle flux, species, and energy crossing the plane where the focal plane is located are all intercepted as outputs; the three simulation outputs are normalized by equivalent observation time as the total output, and this total output can be separately input into the detector mass model as a parallel-beam source.}

   161	\subsection{Prompt atmospheric and delayed-activation source model}
   162	\label{sec:background}
   163	
   164	With the focused signal defined in Section~\ref{sec:optics}, the
   165	background side of the calculation requires two further source layers: the
   166	prompt atmospheric field incident on the payload, and the delayed radioactive
   167	population that this field builds up in the instrument. The two are
   168	constructed separately, so that source generation does not absorb assumptions
   169	that belong to detector selection: everything in this section defines incident
   170	particles or decaying nuclei, while vetoes, topology cuts, and the hard line
   171	window are applied only in Section~\ref{sec:selection}.
   172	
   173	\subsubsection{Prompt atmospheric source}
   174	\label{subsec:prompt_source}
   175	
   176	The background source model is divided into two lines, prompt atmospheric transport and delayed activation, in which the prompt atmospheric particle field is given by EXPACS/PARMA. PARMA3.0 is an analytical model parameterized from PHITS atmospheric-shower calculations and validated against terrestrial cosmic-ray fields, and EXPACS is its public tabulation/software interface \cite{Sato2015EXPACS,Sato2016PARMA4}. PHITS provides the underlying particle-transport physics: primary cosmic rays interact with atmospheric nuclei, the produced secondary hadrons, leptons, photons, and neutrons continue to transport in the atmospheric column, and the final flux is then parameterized by altitude, geomagnetic cutoff rigidity, solar modulation, energy, and direction. In this work, EXPACS/PARMA gives the incident differential flux varying with particle species, kinetic energy, zenith angle, geographic position, altitude, geomagnetic cutoff rigidity, and solar modulation. It is used only to construct the external radiation field; detector response, anticoincidence, topology selection, and mission-time counting are applied in later steps. The reference source definition in this work uses latitude $34^\circ$, longitude $100^\circ$, altitude $38\,\mathrm{km}$, vertical cutoff rigidity $R_c = 11.6\,\mathrm{GV}$, and the solar modulation condition $W = 118.3$ corresponding to 2025-08-31.
   177	
   178	The prompt source includes photons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, protons, $\alpha$ particles, negative $\mu$ particles, and positive $\mu$ particles. For each particle family, the EXPACS/PARMA differential spectrum is converted into a MEGAlib far-field area source, covering the full zenith range $\theta = 0^\circ$--$180^\circ$ and the full azimuth range $\phi = 0^\circ$--$360^\circ$. The zenith-angle dependence is represented by 20 equal-$\mu$ differential angular bins, where $\mu = \cos\theta$, and the solid angle of each bin is $\Delta\Omega = 0.6283185307\,\mathrm{sr}$. The first 10 bins represent down-going particles ($0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 90^\circ$), and the last 10 bins represent up-going or albedo components ($90^\circ \leq \theta \leq 180^\circ$). Both the prompt transport and activation-production transport retain these two hemispheres, so upward albedo particles are not discarded during source construction. It is worth mentioning that the discrete energy spectrum output by expacs skips over the 511keV point, and an atmospheric 511keV background is additionally introduced in the source in the simulation.

   186	
   187	The integrated source-plane fluxes are approximately $4.80$, $0.462$, $0.199$,
   188	$0.117$, $0.112$, $0.0115$, $0.0050$, and $0.0056\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$
   189	for $\gamma$, $n$, $e^-$, $e^+$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\mu^-$, and $\mu^+$,
   190	respectively; the non-photon families together carry about
   191	$0.91\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, or 16\% of the total. If Monte Carlo counts
   192	were allocated strictly in proportion to flux, the detector and activation
   193	statistics of these minority families would be poor, so the non-photon
   194	families are generated with replicated samples and de-weighted in the rate
   195	normalization. All particles are then transported through the
   196	detector/cryostat mass model with Geant4/MEGAlib
   197	\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016,Zoglauer2006}; the prompt run and the
   198	activation-production run each generated 25,210,216 incident primaries. Each
   199	particle species is normalized with its own transported exposure,
   200	\begin{equation}
   201	  r_{\mathrm{event},j}=\left(\sum_i \mathcal{T}_{ij}\right)^{-1},
   202	\end{equation}
   203	where $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ is the transported exposure of subsample $i$ for
   204	species $j$. This per-species normalization is required because the photon and
   205	non-photon streams have different sampling and exposure structures; the
   206	normalization check enforces $r_{\mathrm{event},j}\sum_i\mathcal{T}_{ij}=1$
   207	for every species, so over-sampling changes the Monte Carlo variance but not
   208	the expected physical rate.

   210	\subsubsection{Delayed activation source}
   211	\label{subsec:delayed_source}
   212	
   213	The activation-production stream is not counted as an immediate detector
   214	background. It records instead the isotope, the material volume, and the
   215	production position of every radioactive product. These records are converted
   216	into a fixed day-15 radioactive population using production--decay buildup,
   217	with ground-state half-lives checked against NUBASE2020
   218	\cite{Kondev2021NUBASE}. For isotope $k$,
   219	\begin{equation}
   220	  A_k(t_{\mathrm{flight}}) = P_k\left[1-\exp\left(-\lambda_k t_{\mathrm{flight}}\right)\right],
   221	  \qquad \lambda_k = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2,k}},
   222	\end{equation}
   223	where $P_k$ is the production rate inferred from the activation-production
   224	transport and $t_{\mathrm{flight}}=15\,\mathrm{d}$ for the reference state.
   225	The resulting fixed delayed activity used by the reference construction is
   226	$85.45\,\mathrm{Bq}$.
   227	
   228	The position information does not come from the aggregate isotope store alone.
   229	The standard store records volume names and isotope counts; the
   230	production-position construction adds a custom hook in the Geant4/MEGAlib
   231	stepping-action layer. At the moment an isotope is stored during activation
   232	buildup, the hook writes a production-position record containing the logical
   233	volume, the pre-step position $(x,y,z)$, the isotope identifier $1000Z+A$, the
   234	excitation energy, time, process, and track-ancestry metadata. The fixed
   235	day-15 population is then joined to these records by volume, isotope, and
   236	excitation state, which gives the delayed source access to the simulated
   237	production coordinates rather than only to volume-integrated activities.
   238	Figure~\ref{fig:delayed_position_distribution} in Appendix~\ref{app:delayed}
   239	shows the resulting position distribution: the activity-weighted map is
   240	dominated by CsI-shield activation and $^{128}$I, with smaller contributions
   241	from Cu, Al, W, Mg, and Cs isotopes. It is a source-construction diagnostic,
   242	not a detector-count map; detector response is applied only after the delayed
   243	decays are transported through the common geometry and event selection.
   244	
   245	Whether the production positions are worth preserving can be tested from the
   246	same table. Delayed decays are emitted locally, so downstream attenuation,
   247	active-shield veto probability, and event topology all depend on where the
   248	isotope was produced, and different incident families---with their different
   249	energy-loss, stopping, and capture histories---need not deposit their
   250	activation in the same materials. To quantify this, the weighted production
   251	rows are grouped by incident particle family and material category, and the
   252	activity-normalized category distributions of two families $a$ and $b$ are
   253	compared with the total-variation distance
   254	\begin{equation}
   255	  D_{\mathrm{TV}}(a,b) =
   256	  \frac{1}{2}\sum_c \left| f_{a,c} - f_{b,c} \right| ,
   257	\end{equation}
   258	where $f_{a,c}$ is the fraction of delayed activity from family $a$ produced
   259	in material category $c$. In the fixed day-15 table, neutron-induced products
   260	carry about $82.5\,\mathrm{Bq}$, $\mu^-$-induced products $2.75\,\mathrm{Bq}$,
