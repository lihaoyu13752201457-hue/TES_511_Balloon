     7	\subsubsection{Reference point-source construction}
     8	\label{subsec:pointsource}
     9	
    10	The focused signal stream is defined by a reference astrophysical point
    11	source. As a concrete example of the target class we take the 511\keV{}
    12	emission episode of the microquasar V404 Cygni observed with INTEGRAL/SPI
    13	\cite{Siegert2016V404}; V404 Cygni is a transient point source in the Galactic
    14	disk and serves here only as a reference for the source class, not as a
    15	predicted target flux. The reference flux scale itself,
    16	$\fzero=10^{-4}\phcms$, is the above-atmosphere value motivated in
    17	Section~\ref{sec:requirements}.
    18	
    19	In the optics run, the source is a monochromatic 511\keV{} far-field beam:
    20	photons arrive parallel to the optical axis, as from a point source at
    21	astronomical distance, and are sampled uniformly over the incident aperture
    22	$A_{\mathrm{inc}}$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:laue_aeff}). Three independent seeds of
    23	$5\times10^{4}$ primaries each give $1.5\times10^{5}$ transported photons, of
    24	which 37,256 are diffracted and cross the focal plane; 37,194 of these
    25	(99.8\%) fall within the $r=1.898\,\mathrm{cm}$ Be entrance window, with
    26	focused-spot radii $r_{90}=1.03\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_{99}=1.25\,\mathrm{cm}$.
    27	The within-window crossings form the event list that is replayed through the
    28	Be aperture into the detector/cryostat model with the recorded positions and
    29	directions.
    30	
    31	The normalization ties this replay to the reference flux. At the detector
    32	entrance the focused stream carries the rate
    33	$\fzero\,\aeff(511\keV)\,T$, where $T=0.739$ is the reference 511\keV{}
    34	atmospheric transmission at the day-15 trajectory anchor
    35	(Section~\ref{sec:mission_time}); numerically this is
    36	$1.48\times10^{-3}\cps$ at the reference flux. Equivalently, the generating
    37	run corresponds to an observation time $N_{\mathrm{fp}}/[\fzero\,\aeff\,T]$,
    38	and each replayed event carries the inverse of that exposure as its rate
    39	weight; in the intermediate cut-flow bookkeeping of
    40	Section~\ref{sec:selection} the same sample appears as a unit replay before
    41	this scale is applied. After the common detector response and event selection,
    42	the surviving signal rate is $1.19\times10^{-3}\cps$, corresponding to a
    43	selected effective area of $S_{\wii}/\fzero=11.9\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Because the
    44	optics run contains only source photons, the replayed stream is signal-pure;
    45	all backgrounds enter through the streams of Section~\ref{sec:background} and
    46	the boundary terms of Section~\ref{subsec:upstream_optics_background}.
    47	
    48	\subsection{Prompt atmospheric and delayed-activation source model}
    49	\label{sec:background}
    50	
    51	With the focused signal defined in Section~\ref{subsec:pointsource}, the
    52	background side of the calculation requires two further source layers: the
    53	prompt atmospheric field incident on the payload, and the delayed radioactive
    54	population that this field builds up in the instrument. The two are
    55	constructed separately, so that source generation does not absorb assumptions
    56	that belong to detector selection: everything in this section defines incident
    57	particles or decaying nuclei, while vetoes, topology cuts, and the hard line
    58	window are applied only in Section~\ref{sec:selection}.
    59	
    60	\subsubsection{Prompt atmospheric source}
    61	\label{subsec:prompt_source}
    62	
    63	The prompt atmospheric field is derived from EXPACS/PARMA. PARMA3.0 is an
    64	analytical model parameterized from PHITS atmospheric-shower calculations and
    65	benchmarked against terrestrial cosmic-ray measurements, and EXPACS is its
    66	public tabulation and software interface
    67	\cite{Sato2015EXPACS,Sato2016PARMA4}. PHITS supplies the underlying
    68	particle-transport physics: primary cosmic rays interact with air nuclei, the
    69	secondary hadrons, leptons, photons, and neutrons are transported through the
    70	atmospheric column, and the resulting fluences are parameterized by altitude,
    71	geomagnetic cutoff, solar modulation, energy, and direction. In this work
    72	EXPACS/PARMA provides the differential incident flux as a function of particle
    73	species, kinetic energy, zenith angle, geographic position, altitude, cutoff
    74	rigidity, and solar activity, and is used only to construct the external
    75	radiation fields; detector response, vetoing, topology selection, and
    76	mission-time counting are applied downstream. The reference source definitions
    77	use latitude $34^\circ$, longitude $100^\circ$, altitude $38\,\mathrm{km}$,
    78	vertical cutoff rigidity $R_c=11.6\,\mathrm{GV}$, and the 2025-08-31 solar
    79	condition corresponding to $W=118.3$.
    80	
    81	The prompt source includes photons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, protons,
    82	alpha particles, and negative and positive muons. For each particle family the
    83	EXPACS/PARMA differential spectrum is converted into a MEGAlib far-field area
    84	source covering the full zenith range $\theta=0^\circ$--$180^\circ$ and full
    85	azimuth range $\phi=0^\circ$--$360^\circ$. The zenith dependence is represented
    86	by 20 differential equal-$\mu$ angular bins, where $\mu=\cos\theta$ and each
    87	bin subtends $\Delta\Omega=0.6283185307\,\mathrm{sr}$: the first ten bins cover
    88	down-going particles, the last ten up-going or albedo-like particles. Both
    89	hemispheres are retained in the prompt transport and in the
    90	activation-production transport, so upward albedo particles are not discarded
    91	at source construction. Figure~\ref{fig:expacs_fullsphere_flux} shows the
    92	resulting full-sphere flux field.
    93

    99	\end{figure}
   100	
   101	The integrated source-plane fluxes are approximately $4.80$, $0.462$, $0.199$,
   102	$0.117$, $0.112$, $0.0115$, $0.0050$, and $0.0056\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$
   103	for $\gamma$, $n$, $e^-$, $e^+$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\mu^-$, and $\mu^+$,
   104	respectively; the non-photon families together carry about
   105	$0.91\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, or 16\% of the total. If Monte Carlo counts
   106	were allocated strictly in proportion to flux, the detector and activation
   107	statistics of these minority families would be poor, so the non-photon
   108	families are generated with replicated samples and de-weighted in the rate
   109	normalization. All particles are then transported through the
   110	detector/cryostat mass model with Geant4/MEGAlib
   111	\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016,Zoglauer2006}; the prompt run and the
   112	activation-production run each generated 25,210,216 incident primaries. Each
   113	particle species is normalized with its own transported exposure,
   114	\begin{equation}
   115	  r_{\mathrm{event},j}=\left(\sum_i \mathcal{T}_{ij}\right)^{-1},
   116	\end{equation}
   117	where $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ is the transported exposure of subsample $i$ for
   118	species $j$. This per-species normalization is required because the photon and
   119	non-photon streams have different sampling and exposure structures; the
   120	normalization check enforces $r_{\mathrm{event},j}\sum_i\mathcal{T}_{ij}=1$
